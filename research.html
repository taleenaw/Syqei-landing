SQ-001 Research file

The moth that fools a computer.

Mission 001 is built from real research at The University of Western Australia. Here's what the scientists found, and how students get to test it themselves.

A flat wing that looks curved

The fruit-piercing moth Eudocima salaminia rests with its wings flat. To a bird, and to us, the wings look like a curled, three dimensional leaf. That illusion keeps it alive.

A team at UWA asked a new question. Does the illusion fool computers too? They pointed computer vision at the wings and asked it to rebuild their 3D shape.

It was fooled. The algorithms rebuilt curves and bumps that weren't there, just like our eyes do.

How the trick works

A second study looked closer, at a related moth called Eudocima aurantia. Its wing scales carry tiny reflecting structures. They throw light the way a smooth, curved leaf would. So the shine itself is part of the illusion.

There's a moth hiding on this bark. Look through the lens to see what a computer vision model sees. This is the same scene generator students use in Mission 001.

How students test it.

In Mission 001, students don't just read about the finding. They run their own version of the experiment.

  1. Find the moth

    Students hunt for camouflaged moths, then race a detector on the same images.

  2. See the illusion

    They spin a wing in 3D and watch the curve disappear side on.

  3. Fool the machine

    They paint a flat wing that a depth estimator reads as curved.

  4. Meet the people

    The researchers introduce the question, and the careers behind it.
