Syqei for Schools

Bring real creative technology into your classroom.

Syqei turns real research mysteries into browser-based investigations where students make something, test their ideas and discover how technology works. Our first mission is designed for a 50 to 60 minute lesson, with no student accounts. Across two pilot sessions, 86 of 93 students who rated the Syqei activity liked or loved it.

Everything a school needs to know.

The format, who it's for and what you need, in one list.

Format
A browser mission your teacher runs from one class link. Students work on their own laptops, with a short class discussion at the start and a share at the end.
Lesson length
One 50 to 60 minute lesson. The guide marks what to cut for 45 minutes. Optional coding levels stretch it to a second lesson.
Year levels
Written for Years 9 to 12. Mapped to Digital Technologies in Years 9 and 10.
What students make
A labelled dataset, an illusion design of their own and a field file they download and keep.
Equipment
A laptop or Chromebook per student with a modern browser, internet that can reach syqei.com, and a projector. Nothing to install. A lite mode switches off 3D for older devices.
For teachers
A teacher guide with a minute by minute run sheet, questions to ask and an answer guide. Plus a device check, curriculum notes, a careers library and an onboarding call.
Curriculum fit
Digital Technologies Years 9 and 10, extension and gifted programs, and careers and pathways. See the details
Accessibility
Read aloud on every step, keyboard support, a non-3D option and captions on the researcher video.

Where Syqei fits best.

All four teachers who gave feedback at our October school sessions placed it in extension or gifted programs. Three also placed it in careers.

Extension and gifted

A real research problem with no single right answer. Students design, test and improve their own solution, and fast finishers go further with optional coding levels. It runs as a one-off enrichment lesson or inside an extension program.

Careers and pathways

Digital Technologies, Years 9 and 10

Australian Curriculum version 9 content descriptions. WA schools use the SCSA version, which may number some content differently.

AC9TDI10P06

Validate algorithms and programs by comparing their output against a range of test cases.

Students run a detection algorithm on six camouflage scenes, compare its answers to their own labelled data, and see where it fails.

AC9TDI10P08

Generate, modify, communicate and critically evaluate alternative designs.

Students design several versions of an illusion, test each one against a depth estimator, and keep the version that works best.

AC9TDI10P09

Implement, modify and debug programs.

In the optional coding levels, students edit a small paint function, then find and fix a bug. Good for a second lesson or fast finishers.

AC9TDI10P10

Evaluate existing and student solutions against design criteria and future impact.

Students discuss where computer vision is used in the real world and what it means when it can be fooled.

AC9TDI10P02

Analyse and visualise data to draw conclusions and make predictions.

Students build a small labelled dataset and read a chart of their search times against the computer's. A lighter link, since it doesn't use spreadsheets.

Questions schools ask.

Do students need accounts or emails?

No. Students open one class link. There are no logins, and progress saves in the browser on their own device.

What devices does it work on?

Recent Chrome, Edge, Safari and Firefox on laptops and Chromebooks. Phones and tablets work but a laptop is more comfortable. Open the device check on one student laptop to be sure.

Where does it fit in the curriculum?

It fits extension and gifted programs first, then careers programs and Digital Technologies in Years 9 and 10. See the curriculum fit. We can map it to your program on request.

What data do you collect about students?

No names, emails or accounts. Nothing students make is uploaded. Read the student app privacy notice.

Can a teacher run it without you?

Yes. That's the point. The teacher guide has a minute-by-minute run sheet, questions to ask and an answer guide. Founding schools in Perth can also have us there for the first lesson.

How does payment work?

Schools pay A$18 per student, per year, for every mission. We confirm the number of students first, then invoice after your class has used it. School purchase orders are fine. If a student's school doesn't have Syqei yet, or they're homeschooled, parents will be able to buy home access for A$79 per student for 2027.

What if it doesn't work on the day?

If something on our side stops the lesson running, we reschedule it at no cost.

Do you work with programs outside school?

Yes. Holiday programs, tutoring centres and clubs can license missions for their groups. Tell us about your program.
