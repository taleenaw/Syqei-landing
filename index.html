Technology missions for secondary schools.

Students use real emerging tools to solve real research problems, then meet the people who do it for a job. Each mission runs in one lesson, in the browser, with no logins.

How a mission works.

Every mission follows the same four moves, so teachers know what to expect and students know where they are.

  1. Meet the research

    Each mission starts with a real question from working scientists. Students hear it from them.

  2. Use the tools

    Students run the kinds of tools researchers use. Computer vision, 3D, data and code, all in the browser.

  3. Make something

    They design their own solution, test it and save it to a field file they can download.

  4. See the careers

    Every mission ends with what students can now do and the jobs that do this for real.

Mission 001The Illusion Engine

Some moths have flat wings that look curved. The trick fools birds, people and computers. Students find out how, then design an illusion of their own.

Built from research by
Dr Jennifer Kelley and Dr Annie Jessop
Technology
Computer vision, depth estimation, 3D and code
Careers
Computer vision engineer, behavioural ecologist, biophotonics researcher, technical artist

Decide at 8.15. Run it at 9.

Every mission comes with a teacher guide you can read in five minutes. You don't need a tech background to run it.

Before class

  • Open the mission once on the classroom display so it's ready
  • Open the device check on one student laptop
  • Share one class link. No logins, no accounts

The full guide also has learning outcomes, an answer guide for every step, careers to talk about and notes for IT.

Each track is three missions on one technology. Students who finish one can go deeper with the next.
